The station timetable was posted beside the entrance. The first train left at nine, followed by another service at ten. A traveller checked the platform number, folded the paper map and placed it in a side pocket. The display changed to show the next destination, and a cleaner moved a trolley along the empty corridor.

At the office, the team sorted six boxes of stationery. Each box contained notebooks, pens and adhesive labels. The labels were arranged by size before anyone began counting. One person entered the totals into a spreadsheet while another checked the packing list. When the numbers matched, the boxes were placed on the lower shelf.

The museum guide described how the clock had been repaired. Its case was made from oak, and the numbered dial had been cleaned by hand. A small diagram showed the position of the gears. Visitors could compare the diagram with a photograph taken before the work began. The display included a written account of the tools used.

In the garden, a row of markers identified the vegetable beds. The gardener measured the distance between each marker and wrote the results on a clipboard. A hose was returned to its hook after the containers had been filled. The remaining seeds were divided into envelopes, labelled with the planting date and stored in a dry cupboard.

The library's new shelves were numbered from one to twelve. A catalogue listed the books assigned to each shelf. The librarian moved a stack of reference books from the desk to a trolley and checked every title against the list. A sign beside the window explained how readers could reserve an item for collection the following week.

Before closing, the workshop manager counted the tools and checked the inventory sheet. The drill, measuring tape and spare batteries were returned to their marked places. The floor was swept, the desk was cleared and the delivery forms were filed. The last task was to record the next day's opening time on the noticeboard beside the door.
